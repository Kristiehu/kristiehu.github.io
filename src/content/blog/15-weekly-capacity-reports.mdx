A fibre network's capacity lives in two places. **Inside plant (ISP)** is the equipment in
hubs and buildings: access and core devices and their ports. **Outside plant (OSP)** is
everything in the field: the cables, the splice closures that join them, and the
structures they run through. Planning needs both: which equipment still has free ports,
and where the fibre itself has room.

Putting those reports together by hand took about two days. I built two pipelines on
tools the team already used every day: Google Drive, scheduled jobs, bash and Python.
The reports now take under half a day.

## Inside plant, every week

One script runs every step, every Monday morning.

1. **Collect.** A Google Apps Script drops the latest equipment exports into Drive.
2. **Download.** Only the files that changed in the last two days are fetched, so nothing
   is processed twice.
3. **Build.** Python works out free and used ports for access and core equipment,
   uplinks, device counts, and what was added or removed that week, then formats it all
   into the report.
4. **Publish.** The report goes into a new folder named by date, next to every earlier
   week, and a running summary takes that week's numbers.

Each step writes its own log, so when something fails, the log names the step.

<div style={{ height: "2rem" }} />
## Outside plant, every month

Every month a bash script runs the OSP cycle. It starts from last month's report and
merges in the new splice closure capacity, cable capacity, structure and location exports.
It fills in missing coordinates from the other sources, adds locations that weren't there
before, and pushes the month's changes out as a new report. All the inputs sit in one
config file, so each cycle is one command, not an afternoon of copying and pasting.

## What changed

- **Under half a day instead of two.** The time goes into reading the reports, not
  assembling them.
- **One place to look.** Everyone opens the same shared folder, so nobody has to ask who
  has the latest version.
- **History built in.** Every week and every month is kept, so "how fast is this filling
  up?" has an answer instead of a guess.

Both are part of the automated geospatial ETL I run, which handles 450+ files a month.

Between teams, a daily exchange now
[reconciles records across two teams' systems](/blog/16-cross-team-alignment/). All of it
depends on the records being right in the first place, which is what
[spatial validation of splice sequences](/blog/14_cable-order-check/) is for.

## Next steps

- **One view of capacity.** Bring ISP and OSP together, so planning sees free ports and
  free fibre for the same place at once.
- **Forecasting.** With months of history, estimate when each piece of equipment or each
  cable will fill up, so planning can act before it does.
- **Shared infrastructure.** Move the scheduled runs onto shared, always-on
  infrastructure, with an alert when a step fails.
