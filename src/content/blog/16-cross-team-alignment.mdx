Two teams keep records about the same circuits: ours, GIS Application and Automation,
and a software team whose systems run day-to-day operations. Each team works in its own
tools, and behind them circuit status is refreshed through different APIs at different
frequencies.

That timing gap is normal, but it means the two systems can briefly tell different
stories. A circuit may be in service on one side while the other hasn't picked up a status
yet, or deactivated on one side while the other still lists it as in service. Now and then
a manual entry adds a difference of its own. Keeping the two aligned needs a bridge
between them.

## Why a bridge

A difference that nobody looks for doesn't stay small. Without a regular comparison,
differences build up quietly, and the longer one sits, the more likely someone downstream
works from the version that's behind.

## The exchange

Four steps: pull, compare and send happen on our side; the update happens on theirs.
Because these records carry internal details, this exchange runs over an encrypted SFTP
connection rather than email or a shared folder.

The file is the agreement between the two teams. Neither team has to work inside the
other's systems; the only shared point is one folder on their server.

## What changed

Every day it brings around 20 records back into line, across every property we maintain
in the Greater Toronto Area, Montreal, Vancouver and Ottawa. It has been running every
day since 2025.

Each difference goes to the team that owns the record, close to when it appears, so the
two systems stay aligned day to day rather than drifting apart.

It works alongside the [weekly capacity reports](/blog/15-weekly-capacity-reports/): those
get our own numbers to the people who need them; this keeps two teams' records in line. Both depend on the data being right in the first place, which is what
[spatial validation of splice sequences](/blog/14_cable-order-check/) is for.

## Next steps

- **Measure the loop.** Track how long each difference takes to resolve, not only how
  many are found.
- **Compare more fields.** Extend the check beyond IDs and status to the other details
  both teams keep.
- **Shared infrastructure.** Run the exchange on shared, always-on infrastructure, and
  have the receiving side flag any day when no file arrives.
